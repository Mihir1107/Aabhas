# SIH26170 — headline numbers (Module A, Module B, L5 conformal, decision layer, explainability)

Dataset **`dataset-v1.2`**. See `CHANGED_NUMBERS.md` for every figure that moved
in the final audit and from v1.0. 120,000 parts in 240 lots; 2,100 defective
(1.75%). Lot-grouped split, never row-wise: train LOT000–143, validate
LOT144–191, test LOT192–239. All figures are on **test lots only** (24,000
parts, 427 defective) unless stated. For slides, use `PRESENTATION_PACKAGE.md`;
for the full guide, the documentation site generated by `make_docs.py`.

Defective = I, II, III, IV, Vb. Good = normal parts **plus the Va / VI / VII
traps**, so flagging a trap is counted as yield loss. Operating point is 7%
yield loss (93% yield, the ITC 2020 reference). Accuracy is not reported.

## The numbers

1. **L0, static datasheet limits: 0.0% recall.** Zero on every injected type:
   the strawman, and confirmation that the in-spec guarantee holds.
2. **L1, AEC-Q001 dynamic PAT (MAD): 54.3% recall at 93% yield**, escape 45.7%.
   Union across 5 parameters x 4 checkpoints.
3. **Cumulative C3 (L0+L1+L2+L3): 92.7% recall**, escape 7.3%, at the same 7%
   yield loss. C4 (+L4) is 92.3%, 2 parts fewer: the ladder plateaus at L3.
   Threshold calibrated on validation lots instead: 92.3% at 6.3% yield loss.
4. **Module B, 0 h + 24 h only, MAE on v168:** 0.610 uA (Iddq), 1.298 nA
   (leakage), 0.067 ns (delay), 0.677 mA (supply), 0.842 mV (vth), LightGBM.
   4.7x better than linear extrapolation on Iddq; 5.4x to 16x worse on defective
   parts than on good ones, which is why both are always shown.
5. **Conformal risk control holds at every alpha from 1% to 20%**: no
   statistically significant violation across 40 lot-grouped splits per alpha
   (all p > 0.33). A 1% guaranteed expected escape rate costs 39.0% yield loss
   on the fused detector; 5% costs 11.6%; 10% costs 4.4%.
6. **Decision layer (six tiers), every test part:** 92.0% of defects held for
   review or reject, 1.2% sent to re-test, 1.69% of good parts held, 0 real
   defects excused as fixture suspect; 35.0% of centre-hiders (all lots) reach
   review.
7. **Explainability:** reason correctness 77–100% on defective classes (Type II
   weakest at 76.7%; III+IV pooled 100% on n = 41); completeness 68.8% flagged /
   100% passed; counterfactuals offered for 7.9% of flagged parts, each
   validated against the whole record; stability under re-measurement 90.0%.
   On the traps, 95.0% of Type VI and 51.7% of Va are fully passed.
8. **Negative results, stated:** L4 does not beat L3; LightGBM ties Huber
   (0.6044 vs 0.6103 on Iddq); Module B's 95% intervals deliver 0.926–0.954
   coverage; SHAP and Huber disagree on feature importance (rho 0.617) because
   v0 and v24 are 0.987 correlated.

## What must be said out loud

- **Two dataset artifacts were found and fixed, each with an assertion.** v1.0
  Type III trajectories were 3–5x too smooth (A8). v1.1 pinned shrunk defects to
  exactly `limit − 3%` (A1b). Removing the second cost the ladder nothing.
- **A label leak, measured and excluded.** `PULLED_FAILED` is observable, but
  every pulled part is defective by construction. The same autoencoder with it
  as a feature scores 92.3% instead of 89.5%. It is excluded from every number.
- **We contradict ITC 2020 on Isolation Forest.** 0.0% of Type III; 42.2% overall
  on a narrow 20-feature matrix against 67.7% on the 83-feature one, so it is
  not a dimensionality artifact.
- **Fusion buys recall and costs ranking quality.** Max robust-z fusion reaches
  92.3% recall; LOF alone has the best PR-AUC (0.882), and greedy selection on
  the validation lots picks LOF alone. Fusion for a gate, LOF for a worklist.
- **Type VI has no test-lot parts and cannot be moved there** without halving
  training data. Every Type VI figure is all-lots: static PAT rejects 358 of
  3,000, dynamic PAT 0, at 6 sigma.
- **Small n.** Type III has 60 parts, 8 in the test lots. Per-tier figures are
  all-lots with Wilson intervals.

## Assumptions a domain expert could challenge

1. Pulled parts count as defective in the recall denominator (17% of all
   defectives); excluding them would lower every recall figure.
2. Unsupervised detectors are fitted on known-good training parts; fitting on
   contaminated data instead moves Isolation Forest from 67.7% to 74.0%, so the
   assumption is not doing the work.
3. The 7% operating point is loose for space grade; at 1% yield loss the ladder
   reads C1 28.1%, C2 73.1%, C3 85.7%.
4. The autoencoder is scikit-learn's MLPRegressor fitted X→X (48,12,48).
5. Module B's safe limit uses only earlier lots' 168 h spread, centred on this
   lot's own 24 h median; a lot's own 168 h readings are never used at 24 h.
